The offer in one line

A finished Johnson County Commons Twin, delivered as a single sealed appliance, for a fixed $20,000, handed to Jeff Eden to take forward commercially if he wants it.

$20,000

Fixed, one time. Appliance, mirror, spatial build, filter and surface included.

No monthly, no seat count, no per-record fee.

What is in the box

  • The appliance. A compact inference/serving box, Strix Halo class — Ryzen AI 9 HX 470 tier, 32 GB, ~150 × 150 × 43 mm, under 120 W, runs 24/7, reachable only over Tailscale. Not a workstation, not a rack.
  • The mirror. A full local copy of the ARK Midwest Watch civic holdings for Lenexa and Johnson County — 2,756,484 held records across 89 partial layers, held on disk, not linked. Person-level datasets are indexed as existing and never mirrored.
  • The spatial surface. Every reachable dataset mapped into one 3D coordinate space, so parcels, infrastructure, streets and places sit together instead of in fourteen incompatible viewers.
  • Anonymised presence layer. Existing camera systems keep operating as they do; a filter on top emits counts and movement only — no identity, no faces, no live video feed.
  • Citizen view. Residents see movement and traffic patterns in their zone on a phone, and can upload a photo that returns only a headcount.
  • Sharing parity. Every sharing function people expect from Facebook, available natively — invite, share a place, go together.
  • No pre-arrival loading. Nothing is marketed before a visitor physically arrives. Once there, the surface adds what is good here, what to order, what not to miss.
  • Payment at the point of experience. Pay in place; because delivery cost is near nil, proceeds route to a named local Johnson County charity rather than a platform rake.
  • Concentric safety beacons. Three resident-set zones; a spoken safe word raises a beacon and digitally stamps in-zone feeds, retrievable only on a specific subpoena. Evidence stays with citizens, not pooled centrally.
